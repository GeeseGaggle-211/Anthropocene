# Milestone three — generations, elevation and tile information

This milestone supersedes the four-band elevation presentation and 90-day player turns described in the older notes. The source Drive document and milestone-two notes remain historical references.

## Elevation and contours

Continuous elevation is unchanged and remains authoritative for climate and drainage. Gameplay elevation is an integer 0–10. Marine Ocean/Shallows tiles are level 0; freshwater lakes inherit an above-sea level 1–9; traversable land occupies 1–9; level 10 is an impassable peak. Camps, hunters, supply paths, herd placement/movement and AI all respect peak blocking. Peak land remains land for coastline classification.

After final hydrology, nine configured land-elevation quantiles (12%, 25%, 40%, 55%, 69%, 81%, 90%, 96%, 99%) define level boundaries. This spreads levels across the world without modifying its continuous height field or biome. Equal heights receive the same level. Levels 1–2 are lowland, 3–4 rolling hills, 5–6 ridges/highland, 7–9 mountains and 10 peaks. Contiguous high land retains deterministic ridge connectivity in simulation data when adjacent levels differ by at most two. Existing productivity modifiers apply to these descriptive groups; there is no human population cap.

The renderer draws one inset contour per level at tile heights of at least 85 pixels. Level 0 has no elevation contour. At smaller zoom it draws `ceil(level / 2)` lines to avoid aliasing, restoring the exact count on zoom-in. Equal-elevation neighbors suppress their shared segments, so plateaus, rolling hills and mountain chains read as joined topographic regions instead of collections of individually outlined hexes. At unequal boundaries the higher tile owns the lines; unknown neighbors remain sealed so missing contours cannot reveal fogged elevation. Level 10 also has an X. The former center-to-center ridge spokes are no longer rendered: ridges emerge from the merged elevation topology. Biome fills, rivers on shared boundaries, oasis/freshwater features, food markers and occupants retain separate layers; river channels keep their distinct cyan color and flow widths.

Both tilesets define `elevation.contours` with configurable `Tint`, `Opacity`, `LineWidth` and `Spacing`, plus `elevation.ridge` and keys `elevation.0` through `elevation.10`. Spacing is bounded by tile size so rings cannot invert. Numeric elevation and its description are always available in the inspector and hover panel.

## A generation is one player turn

The default `TurnDays` is 7,300, with 365 days per year: twenty years. The player commits movement, dispatch and a subsistence strategy once. The historical clock advances by the configured duration and the header displays elapsed years. `TurnDays` remains configurable and shorter durations use the same annualized formulas. `BaseDays = 91.25` is now a rate-conversion unit, not a simulated sub-turn.

One deterministic generational pass combines the four seasonal climate profiles into their exact weighted exposure over the configured duration. It computes renewable production, then resolves hunting, fishing, gathering, disconnected-hunter foraging, herd pressure and proportional competition once. The map is advanced once at the end. There is no eighty-iteration world loop. Movement and active fishing are committed once; an established hunter group automatically hunts wild prey that shares its final resolved tile unless it committed to fishing or reintegration. A newly dispatched group cannot act until the next planning phase. The legacy Hunt order remains readable for versioned history but is no longer emitted by the UI or AI. Herd reproduction is applied before the hunting harvest. Up to sixteen hunters exploiting one herd can take only part of its replacement growth: a below-cap herd retains at least 25% of its realized natural increase and therefore still grows. More than sixteen hunters can reduce it toward the larger of the species minimum or 20% breeding reserve. These pressure and retention values are configuration, not assumptions about a particular unit presentation. All claims remain finite, and every surviving herd removes total plant biomass and drinkable freshwater from its occupied tile or adjacent freshwater source.

Practice is normalized by `PracticeDays = 7300` so the longer historical clock does not automatically grant eighty turns of experience. Gathering and Fishing progress share the ordinary person-generation lifestyle budget according to food actually received; lost harvest does not count. Passive shore and lake collection is classified as 75% Gathering and 25% Fishing practice, while an explicit fishing commitment remains fully Fishing. Hunting practice is still derived from food successfully delivered, with a configurable positive learning-intensity multiplier so dangerous, specialized fieldwork can compete with passive camp production without reducing generalist progress. A zero-yield co-located hunt or failed fishing commitment can contribute only the configured small unproductive-practice fraction. Food transfers are aggregated by tile, claimant and kind after resolution; reports retain the real total flows over twenty years. Resolution runs on a worker with a progress screen and disables other state mutation until the result is committed.

Each practice domain has a smooth proficiency ramp. Benefits begin at 25% of that domain's first-breakthrough threshold and reach full strength at 50%. Full Gathering proficiency takes the same human food while causing 50% less land-condition damage; full Fishing does the same for aquatic condition. Full Hunting fieldcraft yields 50% more usable food from each animal killed, improves the hunter's public migration guidance and stops an experienced group from taking animals once its connected tribe can store the recovered food. These are positive-only bonuses: no domain receives a penalty below 25%, and practising one livelihood does not subtract progress from another.

## Growth and food feedback

The configured Paleolithic annual human growth rate is 0.01145. A generation projects continuous exponential growth with `exp(effectiveAnnualRate × years)` and analytically integrates the food consumed by the growing population. The entire supplied tribe—camp residents plus connected hunter groups—is the reproductive base; whole-person additions appear at the source camp. The effective rate is limited by recurring production, surplus and reserves. Full surplus support requires production above consumption by 25%; reserves alone cannot generate growth. Deficits block growth, and the first fed generation after sustained deficits is recovery. Each camp also persists a demographic growth multiplier, currently 1.0; later-era food abundance, health or social systems may alter that input without replacing the food-limited exponential model or adding a population cap.

Every generated camp starts with at least one complete generation of food at its initial population. Climate controls how much can remain afterward: warm, moist sites store about one generation, while cold or dry sites approach four. Excess is reported as discarded storage. Disconnected hunters claim local food for subsistence before consuming up to 2.5 generations of carried provisions; forage never teleports to camp. Finite local production and shared labor establish carrying pressure without a numerical population cap. Starvation grace and fractional deaths are now measured once per player generation.

Camp population has a persisted workforce ledger. During the Paleolithic every resident not explicitly assigned elsewhere is a gatherer, so a larger camp supplies more gathering labor and a dispatched hunter group reduces it. Later systems may add named assignments such as craftsmen by subtracting those people from the derived gatherer remainder. Assignments are additive specializations rather than Civilization-style worked tiles, and every person continues to consume the same food regardless of role. No Neolithic role or effect is active in this milestone.

Wild herd carrying ceilings are species-specific: camels and goats 100, horses 120, and antelope/bison 150. All opening species currently use a configurable 2.01% annual reproduction rate. A well-fed nine-animal herd reaches a one-hundred-head ceiling in six generations. Growth is annualized at the start of the generation, before hunting, using the preceding generation's food and freshwater shortfall. Regional spawn density is weighted by landscape as well as species suitability: grassland contributes 1.35 times the baseline herd density and the current tundra/cold-steppe proxy contributes 1.15 times, reflecting large grazer-supporting expanses. Species-specific habitat weights still determine which animals appear, so this does not turn desert herds into generic grassland fauna. Default openings clamp surviving herds to their ceiling and do not create daughter entities, preventing ecological entity proliferation. Deterministic fission remains an explicit disabled ruleset switch for later systems that need migration or managed-herd division.

Tiles distinguish total edible plant biomass for herbivores from the human-gatherable subset. Existing human `Food` capacities and stocks are unchanged. The configured starting gatherable shares are 25% in woodland, jungle and on river tiles; 10% in grassland; 8% in tundra/steppe-like country; and 5% in desert. Oasis influence blends its surroundings toward 25%. Human gathering removes both the accessible stock and the same amount of total biomass. Herds consume non-gatherable biomass first and reduce human food only if that remainder is exhausted. Depletion does not snap the human stock back to its biome percentage; regeneration preferentially restores a depleted gatherable layer while never exceeding either its own capacity or remaining total biomass. Separate persistent land and aquatic condition values reduce subsequent production when overused and recover gradually; the practice ramp changes the damage caused, not the food received in the current harvest.

Base carcass yields are expressed in the same twenty-year food units as human consumption: goats 60, antelope 80, horses 110, camels 120 and bison 150 per harvested animal. The opening rules apply a configurable 2.5 field-recovery scale before proficiency, reflecting the usable food recovered by a dedicated hunting group over a generation; full fieldcraft adds up to another 50%. Reproduction-before-harvest, food-aware stopping by experienced hunters and pressure-sensitive retention allow small expeditions to harvest without forcing net herd decline, while concentrated or inexperienced hunting can still overexploit a herd.

Hydrology is generated before biome classification, so river networks can traverse arid climate regions. River moisture usually reclassifies the immediate bank as grassland, woodland or jungle while adjacent tiles remain desert; the intended Nile-like result is a fertile ribbon through arid surroundings rather than a `Desert` label on every river tile.

Every tribe has a persisted demographic report: start, additions, deaths, end, net percentage, fractional progress, food produced/received/required/eaten, reserves, inventory losses and the limiting factor. The player sees only their tribe's report. The sidebar and journal explain positive growth, food-limited decline, no sustainable surplus, insufficient reserves, recovery or rounding. Food mass balance is also checked: initial reserves + received food − eaten food − discarded inventory = final reserves.

## Passive coastal food

A land camp automatically includes adjacent Shallows within gathering radius 1. It shares one labor budget across terrestrial, lake and marine gathering. Open Ocean and nonadjacent Shallows are excluded; no boat is required to harvest the immediate coast. Peaks are excluded from gathering. Marine gathering never supplies freshwater and does not make a seawater-only site a freshwater-valid start.

Turn records and demographic reports distinguish terrestrial gathering, passive marine gathering, freshwater/lake gathering, active fishing and hunting. Visible coastal tiles expose food stock, qualitative capacity and renewal. The tile hover reports marine eligibility and whether the player's current camp can gather that tile.

## Stacks and delayed hover details

Occupants have a stable order: camps, hunter groups, wild herds, domestic herds, then stable ID within each group. Up to six occupants render in separate slots with opaque backplates and gaps between hit areas. Larger stacks show an overflow count. Clicking a friendly slot selects that exact group. Clicking unoccupied space within the tile cycles friendly occupants. The click inspector lists every occupant and provides a separate selection button for every friendly group, including overflow. Hunter movement onto a herd tile is sufficient to hunt automatically after simultaneous movement. Tileset switching preserves occupants, slot layout, selection and semantic information.

Hold the pointer on the same currently visible tile for **more than two seconds**, without moving beyond five pixels, to show a fading detail panel. Delay, movement tolerance, fade-in (0.2 seconds) and fade-out (0.15 seconds) are configurable. Tile changes, leaving the map, pan, zoom, order-mode changes, turn changes and tileset changes reset the dwell. The panel flips beside the tile and clamps to the window; long content widens or uses columns. It is painted with labels/textures after map input and never consumes clicks or scroll input.

The panel includes coordinate/id, biome, numeric elevation/impassability, human-gatherable food, total herd forage, accessible percentage, renewal, soil moisture, water source/potability, river sides/flow/width, coastal gathering reach, improvements, every visible occupant and relevant queued friendly orders. Recent extraction includes only the player's own recorded transfers at currently visible tiles. Foreign population/provisions remain unknown. `TileDetails` receives a filtered `AgentObservation`, never the authoritative world. The sidebar remains the persistent click-to-inspect view.

Wild herd and foreign livestock counts pass through `HerdCountKnowledge`. The default thresholds display 1–9 as “fewer than 10” (`<10` on compact badges), 10–100 rounded to the nearest ten with `~` (midpoints round upward), and larger herds in broad hundred-count bins such as `100+` or `200+`. These thresholds are centralized in configuration. The observer carries only the representative estimate and labels, not the raw count; the AI's coarse size score is derived from that same estimate. Only the player's own domestic livestock exposes exact managed counts. Inspector, hover and optional icon badges all reuse these labels. Small stack slots omit a badge when it would crowd the glyph; the full stack list retains each species and estimate separately.

## Saves and reproduction

File signature is now `ANTHROPOCENE-6`; world, generator and configuration versions are 5, and the observation schema is 6. The new filename is `opening-v5.anthro`. This prototype revision intentionally breaks older save compatibility because it adds authoritative environmental condition and workforce state; older files remain untouched and are rejected instead of silently fabricated. Saves retain elevation levels/ridge links, continuous geography, both food layers, land/aquatic condition, species/water/oasis/supply state, workforce assignments, fractional demographic/death state and complete generation reports.

For the current demonstration, select **Standard, Continents, seed 1729, 32 tribes**. Dispatch eight people, commit, then move that group two tiles east and commit. Planning another two-tile eastward move produces the red disconnected tether and matching sidebar warning. Switch tilesets with V and hold over the camp or coast for details. See `Validation.md` for recorded results.
