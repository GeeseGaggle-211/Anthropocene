Which passes share state, and what was actually tested
Draft observation/forecast reads, Prepare reads, and Resolve herd/resource reads repeatedly invoke LazyEcology.ProjectEcology. Their intrinsic land projection is the tested common read model. Seasonal profiles remain on GPU across turns; capital transition, seasonal recovery and endpoint clamp share a lane. Split uses three kernels; fused uses one; coefficient-fused additionally retains annual affine maps for eligible natural land and uses integer exponentiation. CPU decisions and ordered writes remain unchanged.
The CPU-cache control uses identical sharing without a GPU. Every one of its 100 whole-world fingerprints matches the authoritative CPU. It is slower, showing that shared lookup/bookkeeping has a cost before transfers are considered.
The profiler identifies the allocator as the largest Draft hotspot. At Huge turn 50 its scalar instrumented scope is about 935 ms inclusive and 831 ms exclusive of nested hooks. These are diagnostic one-worker timings, NOT the four-worker window averages above. Most allocator cost is not the current-time ecology projection: hypothetical recovery, forecast/portfolio evaluation, dictionary/object traversal and CPU decisions remain. RecoverLandAfterHarvest on hypothetical views cannot reuse a projection keyed only by the current authoritative tile.
Resource preparation first materializes stocks, chooses/reconciles field ownership and budgets, then fixes claims. Tile integration can be independent only after these CPU outputs exist. Ordered receipt/stock commits feed subsequent economy. Fusing all these phases would require a new resident data model and APIs spanning the CPU decision barriers, not just joining kernels.
This study therefore ran a real shared-state subset across consumers; it did not port the entire planner, herd intention pass or resource preparation/commit to GPU.

Interpretation and next candidate
Coefficient reuse makes the GPU arithmetic much cheaper than a literal fused translation, but no whole-turn crossover occurs for either tested map. Kernel fusion does not remove host-side prediction/packing or the CPU consumers waiting on results. The CPU-cache control also loses, so merely reusing results via a concurrent dictionary is not a demonstrated optimization.
The most plausible next GPU research boundary is batched numerical forecast evaluation over a compact shared tile/camp read model, with CPU selection between batches. It targets the larger allocator/forecast workload and can reuse static profiles across many alternatives. That new boundary is not implemented or claimed faster here. It requires an explicit dependency/ordering contract; porting the allocator wholesale or reordering shared budgets is not justified.
Population growth arithmetic remains a small phase; moving it alone is unlikely to affect the late-turn total. Prioritize profiling/flattening the CPU planner and host data path before a broader heterogeneous simulator. Retain this branch as reproducible negative evidence; do not ship its backend.
